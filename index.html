About

I am a Prime Minister's Research Fellow in the Department of Mathematics at IIT Guwahati, where I work with Prof. Partha Sarathi Mandal.

I studied mathematics for my bachelor's and master's degrees, and during that time I grew interested in graph theory and the design of algorithms. My research now sits between these two areas: I work mostly on how mobile agents can coordinate as they move through a graph, and on how drones can be scheduled to deliver parcels. I submitted my PhD thesis, Miles to Walk Before Delivery: An Approximation, in July 2026.

Research interests

Much of my work is on distributed algorithms for mobile robots and agents. These are settings where many simple agents, each with only a limited view of their surroundings, must work together toward a common goal without any central control. I have studied problems such as mutual visibility, where robots must place themselves so that no one blocks another's line of sight, along with gathering, partitioning a region, and covering the vertices of a graph. A theme that runs through this work is how the agents still succeed when their movements are inexact or when some of them fail.

My other main line of work is on approximation and online algorithms for drone-assisted delivery. The question here is how to schedule a set of drones so that parcels are delivered quickly and within the energy each drone carries, often while a truck moves them part of the way. These problems are hard to solve exactly, so I design algorithms that come with a proven guarantee on how close their schedule is to the best one possible, including the harder case where delivery requests arrive one at a time and each choice is final.

Both lines rest on a shared base in graph algorithms and combinatorial optimization. I am also interested in pursuit–evasion and graph protection games.
